# AIT ATAR teaching sequence

Draft teacher-selected sequence, 16 October 2026. Year 11 syllabus from 2026; Year 12 syllabus from 2027. Not an official SCSA outline or completed syllabus audit.

Each unit: 16 teaching weeks × 4 lessons × 55 minutes = 64 lessons / 58 hours 40 minutes, followed by two reserved exam weeks. Each year: two units / 36 weeks including exams. Across four units: 256 teaching lessons. Projects, practice, feedback and revision are included in the 64 lessons. The exam windows add no teaching lessons; the school sets exam duration and conditions. Move those windows to fit the school calendar while preserving teaching order.

Session plans and full lesson resources have separate statuses. This sequence does not imply that planned chapter resources have been authored. All weekly evidence is developed in class; homework is optional consolidation.

## Unit 1: Media information and communication technologies

Year 11; unit weeks 1–18, year weeks 1–18.

| Unit week | Focus | Four 55-minute lessons | Weekly evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Audience, purpose and the design brief | 1. Diagnose prior knowledge and identify the purpose of a community campaign.<br>2. Describe the target audience and turn its needs into design criteria.<br>3. Compare two digital products against the same audience needs.<br>4. Write a short client brief and explain how success will be judged. | Client brief and criteria checklist |
| 2 | Elements of design | 1. Identify line, shape and space in digital products.<br>2. Investigate texture, colour, form and tone using small visual studies.<br>3. Modify a supplied design to change its intended message.<br>4. Annotate the effect of selected elements on the audience. | Visual studies and annotated redesign |
| 3 | Principles of design | 1. Explain balance, emphasis and dominance using contrasting layouts.<br>2. Use proximity and repetition to build unity and pattern.<br>3. Investigate movement and explain the intended viewing sequence.<br>4. Apply elements and principles to a campaign poster and justify changes. | Annotated poster alternatives |
| 4 | Typography and composition | 1. Compare typeface, size, formatting and spacing for readability.<br>2. Construct layouts using a grid and consistent alignment.<br>3. Explore reading gravity and the rule of thirds in a visual composition.<br>4. Review a peer’s layout and revise typography and composition. | Layout, feedback record and revised annotations |
| 5 | Computer components and storage | 1. Identify the input, processing, output and storage needs of a media task.<br>2. Explain the CPU’s role in a computer system.<br>3. Distinguish primary and secondary memory or storage using a workflow.<br>4. Recommend peripherals for a simple media-production brief. | Annotated system diagram and selection rationale |
| 6 | Operating systems and compatibility | 1. Compare desktop, mobile and server systems for different users.<br>2. Compare Windows, macOS, iOS, Android and Linux in a supplied scenario.<br>3. Explain how an operating system manages resources, access and applications.<br>4. Check hardware requirements and justify a compatible hardware/software choice. | Compatibility checklist and recommendation |
| 7 | Graphic and audio formats | 1. Distinguish vector and raster graphics through scaling experiments.<br>2. Compare CGM, SVG, BMP, GIF, JPEG, TIFF and PNG for intended outputs.<br>3. Compare MP3, WAV and WMA using an audio-production scenario.<br>4. Choose graphic and audio formats for a campaign asset pack. | Format comparison and exported sample assets |
| 8 | Organising tools and assets | 1. Design folders, filenames and an asset register for a client project.<br>2. Investigate software installation and update decisions in an assigned environment.<br>3. Compare open source, closed source, proprietary, shareware and freeware licensing.<br>4. Organise an asset pack and explain how the structure serves the client. | Asset register and software choice record |
| 9 | Spreadsheet lookup foundations | 1. Construct a readable spreadsheet table for campaign resources.<br>2. Model exact-match VLOOKUP using a vertical reference table.<br>3. Model exact-match HLOOKUP using a horizontal reference table.<br>4. Choose and test lookup formulas with known and missing values. | Spreadsheet with lookup tests |
| 10 | Sorting, filtering and spreadsheet layout | 1. Sort related records without breaking row relationships.<br>2. Use filters to answer questions about campaign resources.<br>3. Improve spreadsheet composition, labels and layout for a client.<br>4. Complete an independent spreadsheet practice task and correct errors. | Spreadsheet output and correction log |
| 11 | Attribution and publication decisions | 1. Investigate the syllabus concepts of fair dealing, private use and moral rights.<br>2. Distinguish ownership, permission and acknowledgement in publication scenarios.<br>3. Create consistent references for a small set of digital assets.<br>4. Review a campaign asset pack and correct missing source information. | Source log and publication decision notes |
| 12 | Digital citizenship and communities | 1. Compare social-networking types and their features.<br>2. Explore online chat rooms and virtual worlds as communities.<br>3. Analyse cyberbullying scenarios and propose management strategies.<br>4. Explain responsible participation and the effect of technology on work-life balance. | Scenario responses and participation guidelines |
| 13 | Plan the campaign project | 1. Analyse the campaign situation, audience and presentation medium.<br>2. Create a style guide linked to the agreed design criteria.<br>3. Generate thumbnail alternatives and a storyboard or wireframe.<br>4. Review the design plan and annotate the selected approach. | Brief, style guide and approved draft design |
| 14 | Produce and review the campaign | 1. Create the first campaign product from the design plan.<br>2. Prepare supporting graphic or audio assets in suitable formats.<br>3. Check the product against the criteria and collect user feedback.<br>4. Refine the product and explain changes using evidence. | Working product, feedback and revision log |
| 15 | Evaluate and consolidate | 1. Evaluate the product against each criterion and record limitations.<br>2. Demonstrate the product and explain the design and application choices.<br>3. Complete a short-answer practice task across the unit.<br>4. Use feedback to correct misconceptions and improve an extended response. | Project evaluation and corrected practice responses |
| 16 | Revision and exam readiness | 1. Retrieve design concepts and annotate an unfamiliar product.<br>2. Review hardware, formats and spreadsheet decisions in a new scenario.<br>3. Practise an integrated client response under teacher-set timed conditions.<br>4. Review answers and prepare an evidence-based revision plan. | Practice responses and personal revision priorities |

- Week 17: Exam window. No teaching lessons allocated.
- Week 18: Exam window / school-scheduled follow-up. No teaching lessons allocated.

## Unit 2: Digital technologies in business

Year 11; unit weeks 1–18, year weeks 19–36.

| Unit week | Focus | Four 55-minute lessons | Weekly evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Business needs and project planning | 1. Diagnose prior learning and analyse a small-business communication problem.<br>2. Identify client requirements, resources and success criteria.<br>3. Create a plan of action and choose time-management strategies.<br>4. Plan the project evidence and organise an initial collaboration space. | Requirements and action plan |
| 2 | Compression and file size | 1. Explain why file size affects transfer and display.<br>2. Compare lossy and lossless compression using sample files.<br>3. Crop and resample images for specified uses.<br>4. Compare output quality and size and justify the chosen settings. | File optimisation experiment |
| 3 | Output, versions and cloud storage | 1. Prepare assets for online display and for print.<br>2. Design a document naming and version-control procedure.<br>3. Compare cloud storage arrangements for a business workflow.<br>4. Apply version control to a shared resource and restore an earlier version. | Optimised outputs and version history |
| 4 | Maintaining data and systems | 1. Investigate disk clean-up and temporary-file or browser-cache management.<br>2. Explain disk fragmentation and identify when a tool is appropriate.<br>3. Compare anti-malware, virus, spam and spyware controls.<br>4. Apply approved maintenance tools to a supplied test environment and record outcomes. | Maintenance checklist and evidence log |
| 5 | Network purpose and media | 1. Explain the purpose, benefits and drawbacks of a business network.<br>2. Compare wired, optical fibre and wireless transmission media.<br>3. Interpret transmission rates in a business transfer scenario.<br>4. Recommend media for a business layout and justify trade-offs. | Media recommendation with rate reasoning |
| 6 | Network arrangements | 1. Draw and explain a wired star network.<br>2. Plan a wireless network for a small workplace.<br>3. Compare client-server and peer-to-peer arrangements.<br>4. Select an arrangement for a business brief and explain limitations. | Annotated topology alternatives |
| 7 | Network components and a LAN proposal | 1. Explain the roles of a server, router and switch.<br>2. Distinguish a modem and network interface card in an internet connection.<br>3. Design a LAN using appropriate components and transmission media.<br>4. Review the LAN proposal against client needs and correct the diagram. | LAN diagram and client recommendation |
| 8 | Privacy and personal information | 1. Investigate the Privacy Act syllabus context for collecting personal information.<br>2. Analyse use and access decisions for fictional customer records.<br>3. Identify identity-theft risks in a business scenario.<br>4. Propose safe disposal procedures and explain the reasoning. | Personal-information scenario responses |
| 9 | Access, commerce and communication | 1. Analyse the digital divide through resource and application access scenarios.<br>2. Compare online retail opportunities and communication improvements.<br>3. Evaluate implications of 24-hour communication for a small business.<br>4. Discuss business dependence on electronic and visual communication. | Evidence-based business case response |
| 10 | Animation tools | 1. Create a short frame-by-frame animation for a client message.<br>2. Compare a tweened animation with a frame-by-frame version.<br>3. Add and test a button in an appropriate authoring tool.<br>4. Revise the animation against audience and communication criteria. | Animation, test record and revision |
| 11 | Audio and multimedia | 1. Record or import audio and make basic edits.<br>2. Convert and export audio for a specified delivery context.<br>3. Combine media into a small digital product.<br>4. Test the product and justify the selected application features. | Edited audio and multimedia sample |
| 12 | Web authoring and navigation | 1. Create a navigation map for a small business site.<br>2. Explain the roles of HTML and CSS in an authored page.<br>3. Use templates, graphics and hyperlinks to create linked pages.<br>4. Test navigation and repair broken or unclear links. | Navigation map and linked prototype |
| 13 | Responsive design and collaboration | 1. Compare the same page at narrow and wide screen sizes.<br>2. Adapt layout and media to support responsive design.<br>3. Compare online collaboration methods for the project team.<br>4. Review a shared prototype and act on recorded feedback. | Responsive prototype and collaboration log |
| 14 | Build the business solution | 1. Review requirements and adjust the project action plan.<br>2. Integrate web, animation or audio assets into the solution.<br>3. Check the working product against identified user requirements.<br>4. Refine the solution using test results and client feedback. | Working solution and test evidence |
| 15 | Project evaluation and feedback | 1. Evaluate the solution against the brief with supporting evidence.<br>2. Present the product and justify the proposed business network.<br>3. Complete a mixed short-answer and extended-response practice task.<br>4. Correct answers and revisit privacy or access decisions in the project. | Evaluation, demonstration and corrected responses |
| 16 | Revision and exam readiness | 1. Retrieve file-management and networking concepts in a new case.<br>2. Review application features using annotated examples.<br>3. Complete integrated timed practice across the unit.<br>4. Review feedback and set revision priorities for the exam window. | Practice responses and revision priorities |

- Week 17: Exam window. No teaching lessons allocated.
- Week 18: Exam window / school-scheduled follow-up. No teaching lessons allocated.

## Unit 3: Evolving digital technologies

| Unit week | Focus | Four 55-minute lessons | Weekly evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Client needs and Year 11 foundations | 1. Diagnose Year 11 design and application knowledge.<br>2. Analyse a festival-guide brief and identify audience needs.<br>3. Translate client needs into usable evaluation criteria.<br>4. Compare possible digital products and justify a proposed direction. | Diagnostic and client requirements |
| 2 | Relationships in visual design | 1. Analyse how visual elements combine to create emphasis.<br>2. Compare balance, unity and movement in interface alternatives.<br>3. Modify a design to improve communication for the audience.<br>4. Annotate and critically analyse the effect of the revisions. | Comparative design analysis |
| 3 | Hierarchy and navigation | 1. Organise festival content into a logical hierarchy.<br>2. Build a site map and explain the relationships between sections.<br>3. Design navigation controls suited to the chosen medium.<br>4. Test a user task and revise an unclear navigation decision. | Site map, navigation prototype and observations |
| 4 | Interfaces, help and accessibility | 1. Evaluate a GUI against the intended audience and tasks.<br>2. Investigate usability, inclusivity and accessibility through interface cases.<br>3. Design relevant help features for an unfamiliar task.<br>4. Test an annotated prototype and justify an improvement. | Annotated interface and task-based evaluation |
| 5 | Device specifications and usability | 1. Interpret digital device specifications in a client scenario.<br>2. Connect specification choices to the user’s tasks and context.<br>3. Compare mobile devices with another computer system.<br>4. Evaluate the usability of a proposed device and justify a recommendation. | Device comparison and recommendation |
| 6 | Emerging devices and convergence | 1. Research a documented development trend in mobile devices.<br>2. Evaluate whether an emerging device meets a client’s needs.<br>3. | 10 | Publishing systems and templates | 1. Develop a colour scheme and justify an appropriate colour profile.<br>2. Use layers and frames to organise a publication.<br>3. ## Unit 4: Digital technologies within a global society

Year 12; unit weeks 1–18, year weeks 19–36.

| Unit week | Focus | Four 55-minute lessons | Weekly evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Client information needs and evidence | 1. Diagnose prerequisite knowledge using a client information problem.<br>2. Analyse information sources for verifiability, accuracy and currency.<br>3. Identify client requirements and define evaluation criteria.<br>4. Plan evidence collection and a design process for the solution. | Requirements, source evaluation and evidence plan |
| 2 | Recovery and backup | 1. Explain the roles of a disaster recovery plan and audit trail.<br>2. Compare full, differential and incremental backup scenarios.<br>3. Distinguish backup from archiving in an information lifecycle.<br>4. Propose a recovery strategy and test its reasoning against a failure case. | Recovery proposal and backup comparison |
| 3 | Storage, mining and security | 1. Compare a data warehouse, a data mart and cloud storage.<br>2. Explain the purpose of data mining in a client scenario.<br>3. Compare passwords, biometrics, firewalls and anti-virus software as controls.<br>4. Explain distinct roles for encryption, digital signatures and certificates. | Storage recommendation and security control matrix |
| 4 | Web content and management | 1. Evaluate benefits and risks of user-generated content.<br>2. Investigate syllabus uses of Web 2.0 and Web 3.0 with clearly defined examples.<br>3. Explain the role of HTML and the purpose of a CMS.<br>4. Compare content-management features for a client information product. | Content-management comparison |
| 5 | Web standards and compatibility | 1. Investigate W3C’s purpose and the role of web conventions.<br>2. Apply relevant HTML, CSS, graphics, audio and video conventions.<br>3. Review accessibility, internationalisation and mobile-web considerations.<br>4. Test browser compatibility and document the required changes. | Standards checklist and compatibility test log |
| 6 | Forms and online learning | 1. Explain how digital communication can support education.<br>2. Create a small tutorial using online software features.<br>3. Build a form for fictional or non-sensitive sample data collection.<br>4. Test validation techniques and evaluate the learning resource. | Tutorial, form and validation evidence |
| 7 | Protocols and communication standards | 1. Explain the role of TCP/IP in an online communication scenario.<br>2. Compare HTTP and HTTPS in a web interaction.<br>3. Compare the syllabus’s wireless 802.11x and Ethernet 802.3 contexts.<br>4. Choose appropriate communication methods for a client LAN. | Protocol explanation and standards comparison |
| 8 | Secure LAN design | 1. Analyse client locations, devices and connectivity needs.<br>2. Design a LAN using suitable components and connections.<br>3. Add firewall, password and physical-security measures.<br>4. Justify the LAN and evaluate trade-offs against client requirements. | LAN diagram and justified security proposal |
| 9 | Workplace information practices | 1. Analyse personal or sensitive information risks in a fictional workplace.<br>2. Explain the purpose of a workplace code of conduct.<br>3. Draft clauses for work hours, email, internet use and employee privacy.<br>4. Evaluate a monitoring scenario using the proposed code of conduct. | Code-of-conduct draft and scenario response |
| 10 | Global information and cloud issues | 1. Compare online censorship contexts and their effects on users.<br>2. Evaluate cloud confidentiality and document sensitivity in a client case.<br>3. Evaluate accessibility and availability of online applications.<br>4. Analyse how changing web practices affect digital participation. | Comparative global and cloud case study |
| 11 | Global markets and service decisions | 1. Analyse effects of global technology on productivity and access to resources.<br>2. Compare local outsourcing, global outsourcing and in-house production.<br>3. Explain why an organisation might outsource data management.<br>4. Evaluate the consequences of a proposed outsourcing decision. | Outsourcing options report |
| 12 | Service agreements and software evaluation | 1. Explain the purpose of a service level agreement.<br>2. Compare service types and availability commitments in sample agreements.<br>3. Evaluate software usability against defined client requirements.<br>4. Recommend a service/software combination and justify limitations. | Service comparison and software evaluation |
| 13 | Plan and build the client solution | 1. Refine the brief and select a manageable solution scope.<br>2. Prepare a detailed production and testing plan.<br>3. Build the online solution using suitable application features.<br>4. Integrate a form or tutorial and check information handling. | Working solution and project log |
| 14 | Test, evaluate and refine | 1. Run compatibility and validation checks against planned criteria.<br>2. Use a survey and client feedback to evaluate the solution.<br>3. Record self-reflection and identify evidence-based improvements.<br>4. Refine the product and justify related network or service decisions. | Test log, evaluation evidence and revised solution |
| 15 | Integrated response practice | 1. Analyse an unfamiliar client scenario and identify competing requirements.<br>2. Write a justified data and network recommendation.<br>3. Evaluate service, privacy and global-participation decisions.<br>4. Review feedback and improve an integrated extended response. | Integrated responses and correction log |
| 16 | Revision and exam readiness | 1. Retrieve data, network and application knowledge using a diagnostic.<br>2. Review project decisions and evaluate common response weaknesses.<br>3. Complete integrated timed practice across the unit pair.<br>4. Analyse feedback and prepare a revision plan for the exam window. | Practice responses and final revision priorities |

- Week 17: Exam window. No teaching lessons allocated.
- Week 18: Exam window / school-scheduled follow-up. No teaching lessons allocated.
