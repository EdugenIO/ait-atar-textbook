# Student chapter standard

Edition 0.3 begins the student textbook with four Unit 1 chapters for weeks 1–4. These are original AIT teaching materials, using the instructional pattern observed in the CS companion. The materials remain classroom drafts and are separate from the schedule and syllabus audit.

## Required chapter pattern

1. A concrete audience/client problem, learning goals and prerequisite retrieval.
2. Numbered explanations that define concepts before asking learners to apply them.
3. Key terms, a step-by-step worked example and an explicit explanation of the reasoning.
4. A misconception with a correction.
5. A partially supported task, with reasoning revealed after an attempt.
6. Practice that moves from identification through explanation to application or evaluation; every question has an explained answer.
7. An independent transfer task with expected evidence, success criteria and a comparison response that allows justified alternatives.
8. An interactive quick check with explained feedback, a recap and an exit question.
9. Previous/next chapter navigation, printable content and an appropriate workbook or starter resource.
10. A syllabus source and page reference inside expandable teaching notes; this does not certify full alignment.

Connect each student chapter to the scheduled lessons. In-class activities fit within four 55-minute periods per week; do not add the workbook as a second set of compulsory lessons. Keep planned topics visibly separate from written classroom drafts and independently reviewed coverage.

Use diagrams or comparisons where they explain a concept. Never rely on a visual alone: provide text labels and reasoning. Keep controls keyboard-accessible, avoid collecting student responses, and explain feedback in text rather than colour alone.

## Next writing sequence

Develop the hardware/software section for weeks 5–6, then media formats and application skills for weeks 7–10, technology impacts for weeks 11–12 and the project/revision sequence for weeks 13–16. Split broad content areas into readable student chapters as needed; preserve stable URLs and the coverage register when adding chapters.

Before claiming a content area is complete, map every required knowledge and skill item, review technical accuracy, inspect the rendered chapter and trial the tasks within the allocated periods.
