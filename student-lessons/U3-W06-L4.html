2. Understand the lesson ideas

A specification is useful only when you connect it to a client’s work. Compare devices against required tasks, test practical use and separate measured evidence from marketing claims.

Compare mobile and fixed systems fairly

A mobile device can support a volunteer moving around the site. A desktop may suit an office producing the guide with a large display and full keyboard. Neither is universally better. Consider readability, portability, input, connectivity, charging, durability, app support and the user’s abilities. Check how the full workflow will transfer files or hand work to another person.

Use a requirement gate before ranking extras

If a required application is unsupported, an option has not met the brief even if it has more RAM. Eliminate or flag failed essentials before weighing optional benefits. Mark unknown specifications as unknown, not passed. Minimum requirements show a starting point; usability still needs checking with the actual workload and person.

Analyse convergence through a workflow

A mobile device can combine a camera, communication, navigation and document access. That may reduce what a volunteer carries, but can also concentrate battery dependence, distraction or failure in one device. Compare the complete task sequence with using separate equipment. Recommend the arrangement that meets the client’s needs, with a limitation and a fallback.

Language you can use in your explanation
Specification
A stated technical characteristic or requirement of a system.
Usability criterion
A condition used to judge how well a device supports a user task in context.
Compatibility
The ability of hardware and software to work together as required.
Battery runtime
The time a device operates under a particular workload and set of conditions.
Convergence
Combining functions previously associated with separate devices or services.
Development trend
A documented direction of change across products or technology over time.

3. Follow a worked decision

A fictional festival needs a programme, usable navigation and appropriate media. A walking volunteer and a production editor have different tasks. Treat asset approval, accessibility, device evidence and project resources as explicit constraints.

Today’s decision in that case

Recommend a qualified workflow with missing evidence and a representative test. No purchase is required for the exercise.

  1. Identify: Which requirement, concept or distinction does this decision address?
  2. Explain: Which part of the reasoning supports the decision?
  3. Qualify: What condition or evidence would you need before claiming it works in practice?

The decision is a supplied teaching model. Its proposed checks are not your observed results.

Deeper chapter model: Choose for a four-hour volunteer shift
  1. Essential requirements: A fictional schedule viewer needs App R, readable venue details and at least four hours of operation in the trial workload.
  2. Option A: A portable device supports App R and achieved five hours in the supplied trial. Venue text was readable in that trial.
  3. Option B: A fixed desktop supports App R but cannot be carried between venues without another arrangement.
  4. Option C: A portable device has more RAM, but App R support and runtime are unconfirmed.
  5. Recommendation: A is best supported by the supplied evidence for the walking task. B suits a different context; C remains unverified.
  6. Limit: The trial conditions may differ from the event. Confirm charging, environmental conditions and a fallback before deployment.

These are fictional observations, not measurements made for this textbook or real product claims. A justified choice states what the evidence can and cannot establish.

Spot and correct a connected misconception

Claim to examine: The newest device with the biggest numbers must be the best choice.

Compare the correction

Required compatibility, usability and context come first. Extra capacity cannot establish that a task works.

Supported chapter question

An advertised “all-day” device has no stated test conditions in the supplied brief. Can you mark a six-hour field-runtime requirement as passed?

Check the supported reasoning

No. Treat the claim as insufficient evidence for that workload. Check the published conditions and run a representative trial or obtain suitable evidence before marking it passed.

Related practice and explanation

Analyse

How can convergence create both a benefit and a dependency?

Compare an explained answer

Combining camera, schedule and communication reduces separate equipment, but one flat battery may interrupt several tasks. Explain the task-specific benefit and a fallback.

Transfer and test the limits

Change one relevant client condition: audience/access need, output medium, available application, recovery need or resource limit. Explain which part of today’s decision still applies, which part changes and what new evidence you need. Choose a change that matters to this lesson rather than adding an unrelated feature.

Teaching choices and review limits

Use retrieval, explicit instruction, a worked decision, faded support, self-explanation, discussion, independent application, feedback and reflection in the core route. Optional error analysis, interleaving and transfer can replace practice when appropriate. For a learner needing support, narrow the task and retain one scaffold; for a learner ready for challenge, remove the scaffold or vary a meaningful condition. No new accounts, real personal data or purchases are required.

The lesson uses selected chapter readings and a distinct scheduled-task model. Native application operation, approved online collection, representative access trials and independent subject review are not established by the supplied text. Formal assessment follows the school’s approved conditions.
