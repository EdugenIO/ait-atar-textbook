2. Understand the lesson ideas

Business information represents people. Decide what is needed, who should use it and how it will be protected. Also check who could be excluded by a digital-only service.

Protect records through their whole life

Identity theft can involve stolen contact details, credentials or other identifiers. Avoid shared logins, unnecessary exports and public links to customer records. Before disposal, check retention obligations. Covered organisations must take reasonable steps to destroy or de-identify information no longer needed, subject to exceptions. Deleting a shortcut is not secure disposal; copies may remain in backups, cloud accounts or on devices. Use an approved process that addresses those copies and the storage technology.

Language you can use in your explanation
Personal information
Information or an opinion about an identified person, or someone reasonably identifiable.
Privacy
Appropriate handling of information about people and respect for their private lives.
Identity theft
Misuse of another person’s identifying information to impersonate them or commit fraud.
Access control
Rules and mechanisms limiting who can use information or a system.
De-identification
Reducing the risk that data can identify a person; effectiveness depends on context and other available information.
Digital divide
Differences in people’s access to digital resources and their ability to use them.

3. Follow a worked decision

A fictional bicycle shop needs a small communication solution, managed files and a justified LAN proposal. Staff and customers have different access needs. Use disposable working copies and invented customer information.

Today’s decision in that case

Disposal needs retention reasoning and consideration of shared/backup copies. Deleting a shortcut is not evidence every copy is gone.

  1. Identify: Which requirement, concept or distinction does this decision address?
  2. Explain: Which part of the reasoning supports the decision?
  3. Qualify: What condition or evidence would you need before claiming it works in practice?

The decision is a supplied teaching model. Its proposed checks are not your observed results.

Deeper chapter model: Reduce unnecessary booking data
  1. Situation: A fictional repair form asks for name, phone, item description, date of birth and home address.
  2. Purpose: Arrange a counter appointment and send a reminder; no delivery is offered.
  3. Question: Why are the date of birth and home address needed for this purpose? No reason has been supplied.
  4. Revision: Remove unnecessary fields from the mock-up and explain the purpose of the remaining fields.
  5. Access: Allow only staff who need the booking information to access it.
  6. Review: Check legal coverage, notice, use and retention requirements before deploying a real service.

This classroom decision reduces collection in a stated scenario. It is not a determination of a real business’s complete legal obligations.

Spot and correct a connected misconception

Claim to examine: A business being small proves the Privacy Act never applies.

Compare the correction

Coverage has exceptions. Check the organisation and activities against current guidance rather than assuming the turnover threshold settles every case.

Supported chapter question

A customer used an email address for a booking reminder. A staff member wants to add it to a marketing list. What must be checked?

Check the supported reasoning

The new use needs checking against the original purpose, notices, permissions and applicable rules. Do not assume a reminder contact authorises marketing. Refer a real decision to the business’s approved privacy process.

Related practice and explanation

Evaluate

Does an online store being open 24 hours guarantee instant staff support?

Compare an explained answer

No. The business should distinguish service availability, automated messages and realistic staffed response times.

Teaching choices and review limits

Use retrieval, explicit instruction, a worked decision, faded support, self-explanation, discussion, independent application, feedback and reflection in the core route. Optional error analysis, interleaving and transfer can replace practice when appropriate. For a learner needing support, narrow the task and retain one scaffold; for a learner ready for challenge, remove the scaffold or vary a meaningful condition. No new accounts, real personal data or purchases are required.
