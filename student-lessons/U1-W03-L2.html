2. Understand the lesson ideas

Elements are the components you arrange. Principles explain how those components work together to establish priority, connection and a viewing sequence.

Unity, proximity, repetition and pattern

Place related information close enough to form a recognisable group. Repeat heading treatments to connect related sections. Organised repetition can create a pattern. Repetition should support relationships: making every item identical may hide important differences between a heading, a date and a supporting note.

Dominance
The prominence of a component over other parts of the design.
Unity
The sense that parts belong together, supported by relationships such as proximity and repetition.
Pattern
An organised repetition of visual components.
Movement
The way a composition directs a viewer’s attention through the design.

3. Follow a worked decision

A fictional community repair café needs a campaign that helps residents find the time, venue and permitted items. The client approves content; residents use the product. Use invented records and original or approved assets.

Today’s decision in that case

Place time and venue together, then repeat that grouping consistently. Proximity and repetition can support unity in different ways.

  1. Identify: Which requirement, concept or distinction does this decision address?
  2. Explain: Which part of the reasoning supports the decision?
  3. Qualify: What condition or evidence would you need before claiming it works in practice?

The decision is a supplied teaching model. Its proposed checks are not your observed results.

Deeper chapter model: Explain the relationship, not just the label
  1. Elements: A large heading uses dark tone and generous surrounding space.
  2. Principle: Its size, tonal difference and separation create emphasis and establish dominance.
  3. Audience need: Viewers should recognise the event before reading the details.
  4. Evaluation: Ask viewers what they noticed first and whether they could then locate the date.

“The heading has dominance” is a starting point. A stronger explanation identifies the elements that establish it and why that priority matters in this context.

More support or challenge · choose if needed

Spot and correct a connected misconception

Claim to examine: Movement means the product must contain animation.

Compare the correction

Movement can describe the path of attention through a still composition. Animation is one possible application technique, not the definition of this principle.

Supported chapter question

A poster has three information sections with different heading fonts and equal gaps between every item. Suggest changes that could improve unity.

Check the supported reasoning

Repeat a consistent heading treatment for related sections and adjust proximity so each heading is grouped with its own information. Explain which parts belong together. Do not assume every font must be identical if a meaningful hierarchy needs distinction.

Related practice and explanation

Analyse

Why might six equally dominant headings make a poster harder to use?

Compare an explained answer

They compete for attention and may hide the intended priority. Adjust their relationships so the main message is recognised before supporting information.

Distinguish

How do repetition and proximity support unity in different ways?

Compare an explained answer

Repetition connects components through recurring visual treatment. Proximity connects them by grouping related items spatially. Both can help the viewer recognise relationships.

Transfer and test the limits

Change one relevant client condition: audience/access need, output medium, available application, recovery need or resource limit. Explain which part of today’s decision still applies, which part changes and what new evidence you need. Choose a change that matters to this lesson rather than adding an unrelated feature.

Teaching choices and review limits

Use retrieval, explicit instruction, a worked decision, faded support, self-explanation, discussion, independent application, feedback and reflection in the core route. Optional error analysis, interleaving and transfer can replace practice when appropriate. For a learner needing support, narrow the task and retain one scaffold; for a learner ready for challenge, remove the scaffold or vary a meaningful condition. No new accounts, real personal data or purchases are required.
