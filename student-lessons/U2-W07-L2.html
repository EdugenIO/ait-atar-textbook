2. Understand the lesson ideas

A network connects devices so people can share information and services. Plan from the business’s rooms, tasks and users, then explain the connections and their limits.

Draw the connections and label their roles

In a wired star layout, each wired endpoint connects to a central switch. The switch links local devices; a router links the LAN to another network. A network interface provides each device’s connection. An access point connects wireless devices into the LAN. A modem converts signals for a particular service; a fibre service may use an optical network terminal instead. Home or business equipment may combine several roles in one box. Show functions clearly instead of assuming every role needs a separate purchase.

Explore this concept and its visual/example support

Language you can use in your explanation
Network
Connected devices that exchange data and share services.
LAN
A local area network connecting devices in a limited area, such as a workplace.
Switch
A device that connects devices within a network and forwards traffic towards the intended connection.
Router
A device that forwards data between networks.
Network interface card
Hardware providing a device’s network connection; it may be built in.
Access point
A device providing a wireless connection into a network.

Deeper chapter model: Connect a fictional print shop
  1. Needs: Three fixed workstations, one shared printer and a mobile staff laptop.
  2. Wired links: Connect the fixed devices to a central switch using suitable copper links.
  3. Wireless link: Connect a staff access point to the LAN for the laptop, subject to a coverage check.
  4. Services: Place a file server on the LAN if central file service and access management are required. Plan backup separately.
  5. Outside connection: Use a router at the internet boundary, with service equipment appropriate to the connection.
  6. Review: Check capacity, access controls, installation constraints and an outage/recovery plan.

The recommendation follows the tasks. It combines a physical star with a possible client-server service arrangement.

More support or challenge · choose if needed

Spot and correct a connected misconception

Claim to examine: A star network and a client-server network are competing choices.

Compare the correction

One describes connection layout; the other describes service organisation. A LAN can use both.

Supported chapter question

An 80 MB file crosses a link at an ideal constant 20 Mb/s. Calculate the ideal time and name one reason the real transfer could take longer.

Check the supported reasoning

80 × 8 = 640 megabits; 640 ÷ 20 = 32 seconds. Overhead, congestion or a slower part of the route could increase the time. This is an ideal estimate, not a measured result.

Related practice and explanation

Apply

Why might fixed desks use cable while a roaming laptop uses wireless?

Compare an explained answer

The desks do not need mobility and can have dedicated physical links. Wireless supports movement, but coverage and shared capacity need checking.

Trace

What ideal time does a 10 MB file need at 8 Mb/s?

Compare an explained answer

10 × 8 = 80 megabits. 80 ÷ 8 = 10 seconds before real-world overhead or other limits.
