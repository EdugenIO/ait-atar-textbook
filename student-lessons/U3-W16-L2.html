2. Understand the lesson ideas

Digital production involves rights, responsibilities and other people. Check the basis for reuse and publication, understand the limits of information access and choose collaboration tools that fit the work.

Identify which right is in question

A festival publication can contain text, photographs, artwork, code and commissioned assets. Copyright and design rights answer different legal questions. Copyright concerns protected expression and specified uses; Australian design rights concern qualifying product appearance and have registration requirements. In Australia, a registered design must also be examined and certified before its owner can enforce it against infringement. Do not assume that a digital layout is automatically a registered design or that owning an idea gives every possible right. Identify the asset, proposed use and relevant evidence before deciding.

Choose collaboration for the task

Physical meetings can support immediate discussion and shared equipment, but depend on place and travel. Virtual tools can support distance and a record of comments, but bring connectivity, access, coordination and privacy needs. Compare synchronous discussion with asynchronous review. Agree file ownership, permissions, versions, response windows and how decisions are approved. A hybrid workflow can combine methods rather than treating one as universally better.

Explore this concept and its visual/example support

Evaluate digital change and convergence

A shared mobile tool may combine capture, messaging, editing and publishing. This can speed up a workflow while concentrating access, battery and account dependencies. Compare benefits with the actual team’s abilities, connectivity and review requirements. A faster publication process can spread an unchecked asset or allegation faster too. Preserve review steps that address rights and accuracy.

Language you can use in your explanation
Intellectual property
Legal rights relating to certain creative and intellectual outputs.
Copyright
Protection for specified uses of protected expression, subject to exceptions; it does not simply protect every underlying idea.
Design right
A form of protection relating to the overall visual appearance of a new and distinctive product, subject to registration and legal requirements.
Online defamation
Publication online that harms an identifiable person’s reputation and meets the applicable legal requirements.
Freedom of information
A legal framework for requesting access to specified government-held documents, subject to conditions and exemptions.
Virtual collaboration
Working together through digital tools rather than only sharing a physical location.

3. Follow a worked decision

A fictional festival needs a programme, usable navigation and appropriate media. A walking volunteer and a production editor have different tasks. Treat asset approval, accessibility, device evidence and project resources as explicit constraints.

Today’s decision in that case

Separate publication rights, collaboration access and workflow effects. One decision does not clear the others.

  1. Identify: Which requirement, concept or distinction does this decision address?
  2. Explain: Which part of the reasoning supports the decision?
  3. Qualify: What condition or evidence would you need before claiming it works in practice?

The decision is a supplied teaching model. Its proposed checks are not your observed results.

Deeper chapter model: Separate three decisions about a festival document
  1. Situation: A team receives a digital report from an Australian Government agency through an FOI process.
  2. Access: The access decision concerns which document or parts can be released under that framework.
  3. Reuse: The team separately checks whether it can reproduce a photograph from the report in a public guide. Access alone does not settle this.
  4. Publication: The team checks the accuracy and implications of a proposed statement about an identifiable person.
  5. Collaboration: One person records sources and permissions; another reviews claims before an approved version is shared.
  6. Limit: Unclear legal questions are referred for appropriate advice. No real request or public post is needed for the practice task.

Access, reuse and publication are related but separate checks. One approval should not be silently treated as all three.

More support or challenge · choose if needed

Spot and correct a connected misconception

Claim to examine: An FOI release gives unrestricted permission to republish everything in the document.

Compare the correction

Access rights and publication rights are separate questions. Check the proposed reuse and other applicable obligations.

Supported chapter question

A team credits a photograph in an FOI-released report and plans to reuse it. What remains to be checked?

Check the supported reasoning

Check the rights, terms and other obligations for the intended reuse, including whether the photograph is third-party content. Credit alone and access to the report do not establish unrestricted publication rights.

Related practice and explanation

Analyse

Why is “it was already online” not a complete response to a harmful allegation?

Compare an explained answer

Repeating material can be a new publication and extend harm. Accuracy, identification, context and applicable rules still require checking.

Apply

Does Commonwealth FOI mean you can demand all files from a private shop?

Compare an explained answer

No. Its scope concerns specified government-held documents and is subject to limits. Other bodies or jurisdictions may have different frameworks.

Transfer and test the limits

Change one relevant client condition: audience/access need, output medium, available application, recovery need or resource limit. Explain which part of today’s decision still applies, which part changes and what new evidence you need. Choose a change that matters to this lesson rather than adding an unrelated feature.

Teaching choices and review limits

Use retrieval, explicit instruction, a worked decision, faded support, self-explanation, discussion, independent application, feedback and reflection in the core route. Optional error analysis, interleaving and transfer can replace practice when appropriate. For a learner needing support, narrow the task and retain one scaffold; for a learner ready for challenge, remove the scaffold or vary a meaningful condition. No new accounts, real personal data or purchases are required.
