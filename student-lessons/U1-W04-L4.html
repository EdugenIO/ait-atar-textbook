2. Understand the lesson ideas

Clear type and a deliberate layout help readers find information. Learn to use typography, alignment and compositional rules with a reason for each choice.

Annotate and revise

An annotation explains a design choice. Name what you changed, explain what you want it to do, and link that effect to the brief. Record feedback separately from your intention. If viewers repeatedly miss the venue, revise its position, type treatment or surrounding space and check again. Use what you found to improve the design, then test it again.

Explore this concept and its visual/example support

Language you can use in your explanation
Typeface
A family of letter designs with related visual characteristics.
Alignment
The positioning of components relative to an edge, axis or grid.
Spacing
The distance within or between text and other components.
Grid
A framework of alignment lines used to organise a composition.
Rule of thirds
A compositional guide dividing an area into three rows and three columns.
Reading gravity
A common left-to-right, top-to-bottom reading tendency in Western layouts.

Deeper chapter model: Improve a weak annotation
  1. Weak: “I used a large font because it looks better.”
  2. Specific choice: “The event title is larger than the supporting information.”
  3. Effect: “This establishes a first point of attention, while consistent left alignment connects the date and venue.”
  4. Purpose and check: “Viewers should recognise the event and locate its practical details; test this at the intended viewing size.”

The improved explanation identifies typography and alignment, describes their relationship and proposes an evidence-based check.

Spot and correct a connected misconception

Claim to examine: The rule of thirds guarantees the best layout.

Compare the correction

It is a compositional guide for generating and analysing alternatives. A clear grid or another arrangement may better serve the audience and content.

Supported chapter question

A mobile poster uses centred paragraphs, tight line spacing and a very small venue label. Choose two changes and explain how you would check them.

Check the supported reasoning

For example, use a consistent left alignment for multi-line practical information and increase the separation or prominence of the venue label. Inspect the actual output and ask a viewer to find the venue. Record whether the changes address the observed difficulty.

Related practice and explanation

Distinguish

How is typeface different from size?

Compare an explained answer

Typeface describes the letter design family; size describes how large the letters are displayed or printed. Both influence the appearance and readability of text.

Apply

How could a grid help when the date, image and heading appear disconnected?

Compare an explained answer

Choose common alignment lines and consistent margins, then position related components against them. Check that the resulting groupings express the information relationships.

Transfer and test the limits

Change one relevant client condition: audience/access need, output medium, available application, recovery need or resource limit. Explain which part of today’s decision still applies, which part changes and what new evidence you need. Choose a change that matters to this lesson rather than adding an unrelated feature.
