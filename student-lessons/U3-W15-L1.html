1. Recall before reading

  • Why is completing a task different from meeting a requirement?

Compare with the key terms and selected reading below. Mark one point you changed and explain why.

2. Understand the lesson ideas

A detailed plan connects a client brief to the work, resources and evidence needed to deliver it. Choose an approach, make dependencies visible and respond to changes without losing the purpose.

Evaluate with a traceable record

For each criterion, keep the expected result, context/version, actual observation, revision and retest. Demonstrate the work using the agreed user tasks and explain the approach and resource choices. State passed, failed and untested areas separately. Hand over source and delivery files, asset records, known limitations and next actions. A confident presentation does not replace evidence that the result meets the brief.

3. Follow a worked decision

A fictional festival needs a programme, usable navigation and appropriate media. A walking volunteer and a production editor have different tasks. Treat asset approval, accessibility, device evidence and project resources as explicit constraints.

Today’s decision in that case

Demonstrate representative user tasks and the related evidence. Separate achieved, failed and untested criteria.

  1. Identify: Which requirement, concept or distinction does this decision address?
  2. Explain: Which part of the reasoning supports the decision?
  3. Qualify: What condition or evidence would you need before claiming it works in practice?

The decision is a supplied teaching model. Its proposed checks are not your observed results.

Deeper chapter model: Schedule parallel work without skipping dependencies
  1. Day 1: Confirm the fictional brief and requirements.
  2. Day 2: Draft/review the prototype and prepare approved assets in parallel, using different available people.
  3. Days 3–4: Build only after both the prototype and required assets are ready.
  4. Day 5: Test the delivered prototype against the criteria.
  5. Day 6: Revise and retest the identified issues. This is an illustrative estimate, not a promise of fixed completion.
  6. Change case: If required assets are delayed, the dependent build may move unless a reviewed alternative or partial build is feasible.

Parallel tasks can save calendar time only when dependencies and resource capacity allow them.

Use the chapter visual walkthrough to sketch or label the important relationships. The picture supports your explanation; it is not a replacement for checking the claim.

More support or challenge · choose if needed

Spot and correct a connected misconception

Claim to examine: A Gantt chart with neat bars proves the schedule is achievable.

Compare the correction

The plan also needs realistic durations, dependencies, people, resources and review time. A date on a chart is not evidence that a task has finished.

Supported chapter question

The prototype is approved, but a required asset is not ready. Can the full build automatically start on its original date?

Check the supported reasoning

No. Check which build tasks depend on that asset. Unblocked work may proceed with a reviewed placeholder or alternative, but update the plan and make clear what is still awaiting approval.

Related practice and explanation

Compare

Why might an uncertain navigation task benefit from a prototype approach?

Compare an explained answer

A trial interface lets the client and users test an idea before committing to all production work. Define review and scope controls so feedback can lead to manageable revisions.

Choose

Which representation best describes a decision such as “session full: choose another session or return”?

Compare an explained answer

A flow chart can show the decision and its branches. A site map describes destinations rather than the full decision process.

Transfer and test the limits

Change one relevant client condition: audience/access need, output medium, available application, recovery need or resource limit. Explain which part of today’s decision still applies, which part changes and what new evidence you need. Choose a change that matters to this lesson rather than adding an unrelated feature.

Teaching choices and review limits

Use retrieval, explicit instruction, a worked decision, faded support, self-explanation, discussion, independent application, feedback and reflection in the core route. Optional error analysis, interleaving and transfer can replace practice when appropriate. For a learner needing support, narrow the task and retain one scaffold; for a learner ready for challenge, remove the scaffold or vary a meaningful condition. No new accounts, real personal data or purchases are required.

The lesson uses selected chapter readings and a distinct scheduled-task model. Native application operation, approved online collection, representative access trials and independent subject review are not established by the supplied text. Formal assessment follows the school’s approved conditions.
