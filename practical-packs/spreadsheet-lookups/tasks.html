Predict, test and explain

Use your own copy of the editable workbook. Record expected and observed results in your notebook or a copy of this sheet. The source data are fictional.

1. Trace the worked examples

  1. Construct a small campaign resource table in your own sheet using Code, Asset and Quantity headings. Keep codes as text and quantities as numbers; use readable labels and number formats.
  2. Read Worked lookups A8:C10. Predict the four results for E8:E11, then compare F8:F11.
  3. Explain why the range starts with the key column and why the return index is 3.
  4. Predict the results of changing C8 to 15. Test both the vertical and horizontal examples, record what changed and restore 12.
  5. In the horizontal formula G18, explain why the row index is 2 even though the quantity row is worksheet row 19.

Evidence: predictions, observations and an explanation of the range/index.

2. Investigate a plausible wrong answer

  1. On Investigate, identify every occurrence of A02. Compare its raw result with its Matches count.
  2. Swap the complete A9:C9 and A11:C11 records. Predict what happens to the A02 lookup. Check and restore both records.
  3. Compare X99 with Z00. Explain why replacing every #N/A with zero would hide a problem.
  4. Compare P01 with P01 plus a trailing space. Inspect the formula bar to see the difference.
  5. Try a blank test cell. Explain why it needs an input, rather than reporting a successful zero quantity.
  6. Describe how you would establish the authoritative record before correcting the duplicate.

Evidence: one changed-order test and explanations for duplicate, missing, zero, space and blank cases.

3. Keep records together

  1. Save a recovery copy. On Sort and filter, click inside the table and sort Quantity from smallest to largest. Confirm that the whole table is selected or expanded; never sort only the Quantity column.
  2. List the resulting code order and check each code's asset, medium and quantity against the original CSV.
  3. Use the Quantity filter to show values greater than 10. Record visible codes, visible quantities, visible record count and all quantities.
  4. Explain why SUM and SUBTOTAL now answer different questions.
  5. Clear the filter. Confirm that six records return and that no source rows were deleted.

Evidence: a sorted copy, a filtered-view record and a successful clear-filter check.

4. Work independently

  1. On Practice, predict results in G8:G13 before entering formulas.
  2. Write an exact VLOOKUP in F8 using E8 and source A8:C11; anchor the source and fill down to F13.
  3. Explain the L04, L99 and trailing-space results separately.
  4. In G20, write an exact HLOOKUP using F20 and B19:E20. Test L03, L04 and L99.
  5. Explain one correction you made, then change a valid source quantity and demonstrate recalculation.
  6. Restore the source quantities and save the workbook as a new .xlsx. Keep the formulas editable.

Evidence: completed workbook, predictions, test results and one explanation of a correction.

Review after your attempt

Open the explained answers. Different wording is acceptable when it explains the same data relationships. An observed result should describe what your application actually did.
