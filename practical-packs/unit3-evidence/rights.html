Separate ownership, access and publication decisions

Unit 3 · fictional community-festival client brief. Read the chapter explanation first. These are worked decisions, not observed learner results.

Worked decisions

FocusDecision and explanation
IP and purposeIntellectual property includes distinct rights. Copyright concerns specified uses of protected expression, while a design right concerns qualifying product appearance. Their purposes include giving creators/owners control under their respective schemes; credit alone is not universal reuse permission.
Asset decisionA fictional contributor supplies a photograph for a classroom draft only. A public festival page is a different use. Keep the stated terms and seek the needed permission through the teacher; do not treat folder access as permission.
Defamation caseA draft makes an unsupported accusation about an identifiable fictional organiser. Reputation harm, publication context, legal requirements and possible defences matter; being offensive alone does not decide liability. Pause the allegation and preserve an appropriate review record.
Response routesRemoval/correction requests, specialist advice and potentially civil proceedings are different routes. Court action may seek compensation; entitlement, procedure and outcome depend on the case. The exercise does not contact anyone or predict a legal result.
FOI scopeFor a fictional Australian Government report, the Commonwealth FOI framework concerns access to held documents, with exemptions and review routes. It is not a right to every private shop file. Make the scope clear and follow the applicable procedure.
Access versus reuseA report released under FOI can still contain material needing a separate reuse decision. Check the proposed excerpt/image, applicable terms and publication concerns rather than assuming release clears everything.

Guidance checked 27 October 2026: IP Australia · Attorney-General’s Department · Legal Aid WA · OAIC FOI.

Check your reasoning

A government report has been released. Does that automatically authorise a festival poster using every photograph?

Compare your explanation

No. Access and the proposed reproduction are separate decisions; inspect the relevant rights and conditions.

Production sequence

  1. Create a register for three invented festival assets/claims: creator/source, proposed use, terms, missing permission and reviewer.
  2. Classify a photograph reuse question, a product-appearance question and an unsupported fictional allegation. Explain the distinct rights/issues and evidence needed.
  3. Draft a hypothetical, narrowly scoped Commonwealth FOI document description and one possible limit/review route. Do not submit a request.
  4. Record an editorial decision for each asset/claim and explain why access, authorship credit and publication permission are separate.

Apply independently

Complete the production sequence using your own version of the festival brief. Change the venue and one user access need, then justify and test the resulting revision. Retain your own outputs and observations; the supplied reference is a model.

Use this within the existing chapter task, not an extra assessed project or additional teaching week. Record expected and actual results separately.
