Worked decisions

FocusDecision and explanation
Brief and approachThe fictional client wants a readable guide for two sessions, with an offline option. Record requirements, constraints, deliverables, criteria, roles and approvals. Prototype the uncertain navigation; use structured checkpoints for approved content and production. Neither approach guarantees success.
Purposeful drawingsA storyboard sequences a media introduction. A site map relates programme and session destinations. A flow chart describes a visitor decision. A visual layout positions heading, controls and content. Label assumptions so a reviewer can challenge them.
Worked scheduleSix illustrative day blocks: D1 confirm brief; D2 prototype review and asset preparation with two different available people; D3–4 build after both; D5 test; D6 revise/retest. These are estimates, not observed progress or extra teaching weeks.
Resource counterexampleIf one person must do both D2 tasks, they cannot both occupy that person for the same full day. Move one task or agree another resourcing/scope option, then update dependent build and handover dates.
Tools and managementUse approved project software, a board or spreadsheet to record owner, duration, predecessors, status and decisions. Gantt bars show periods; predecessor logic and resource checks establish whether the proposed plan is feasible.
Evaluate distinct questionsStructure: information relationships. UI: controls/presentation. Usability: task performance. Accessibility: disability-related barriers. UX: broader perceptions/responses. Retain evidence and limits for each rather than one “looks good” verdict.

No. Identify which work depends on that asset. Continue unblocked work only with an agreed plan; update dependencies and record the unresolved permission.

Production sequence

  1. Develop your detailed brief with the teacher acting as client; define observable success criteria and the approval record.
  2. Compare prototype and structured approaches, choose a justified approach and produce annotated draft layouts with approved layout tools.
  3. Create a storyboard, site map and decision flow chart where they resolve different planning questions. Use the linked reference as a model, not your own finished work.
  4. Create a Gantt/task plan with estimates, dependencies, owners, milestones and a resource-capacity check. Record a changed access/venue requirement and revise the plan.
  5. Build your product under the plan, update actual progress, then test criteria, revise and retest. Hand over source/delivery files, rights records and known limitations.

Apply independently

Complete the production sequence using your own version of the festival brief. Change the venue and one user access need, then justify and test the resulting revision. Retain your own outputs and observations; the supplied reference is a model.

Use this within the existing chapter task, not an extra assessed project or additional teaching week. Record expected and actual results separately.

Evidence to retain

  • Keep brief versions, drawings, schedule, changes and actual progress.
  • Trace a design choice to a criterion and its observed check.
  • Do not mark a task complete merely because its planned date passed.

Actual learner performance, native application output and independent teacher review remain unverified. Supplied model results must not be copied into an observed-result log.
