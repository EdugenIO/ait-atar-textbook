Choose recovery sets and match controls to risks

Unit 4 · fictional community-centre client brief. Read the chapter explanation first. These are worked decisions, not observed learner results.

Worked decisions

FocusDecision and explanation
Client recovery needBookings are the fictional centre’s priority. Specify tolerable loss and interruption, owners, recovery order, protected copies and a representative restore test. A plan is broader than choosing backup software.
Worked restore chainF is Sunday’s full backup; I1 is Monday’s changes since F; I2 is Tuesday’s changes since I1. Tuesday incremental recovery needs F + I1 + I2 in order. Tuesday differential D2 includes changes since F, so use F + D2. Neither I2 alone nor D2 alone establishes a complete restore.
Archive and auditAn archive supports longer-term retention/reference; it is not automatically a current recovery copy. An audit trail records actions and times; permissions, review and protection of the log matter. It does not restore missing content by itself.
Storage purpose/locationA warehouse combines data for wider reporting; a mart focuses on a subject or department. Cloud describes a service/location arrangement, not a guarantee of an analytical structure or independent backup. A warehouse or mart can be hosted in the cloud.
Mining and secure processingMining searches for patterns; a correlation in bookings does not establish its cause or justify collecting more personal information. Apply appropriate access and security controls during processing as well as storage. The map flags the source indentation around this group for subject review.
Password / firewall / biometric / anti-virusPasswords support authentication; a firewall applies traffic rules; biometrics use physical/behavioural traits in an authentication process; anti-virus detects/blocks some malicious software. None alone supplies every access, recovery or confidentiality requirement.
Encryption / signature / certificateEncryption protects confidentiality when keys and endpoints are handled appropriately. A digital signature supports integrity and signer verification; it does not automatically hide the message. A digital certificate binds a public key to an asserted identity under a trust framework; validate that trust rather than treating the word certificate as proof of content truth.
