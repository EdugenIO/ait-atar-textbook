Read an SLA and compare outsourcing on equal terms

Unit 4 · fictional community-centre client brief. Read the chapter explanation first. These are worked decisions, not observed learner results.

Worked decisions

FocusDecision and explanation
Agreement scopeAn SLA records agreed service expectations, measurement, responsibilities and remedies. Identify service type, covered functions, operating window, exclusions, support and review. An availability target is not a promise that every request will complete instantly.
Fictional availability modelAssume a 30-day, 24-hour measurement window and no excluded maintenance: 30 × 24 × 60 = 43,200 minutes. A 99.5% target allows 0.5% = 216 minutes of counted unavailability in this simple model. The hypothetical 180-minute outage gives (43,200 − 180)/43,200 × 100 = 99.5833%.
Limits and supportIf only supported business hours count, the denominator changes. Confirm what downtime is counted and what evidence resolves disputes. A 30-minute support response target concerns acknowledgement/action as defined; it does not automatically mean service restoration within 30 minutes.
Same-scope outsourcing comparisonCompare in-house control/skills/capacity with local external support and global specialist capacity. Include handover, communication, data handling, continuity and total scope/cost. Time-zone or price differences alone do not decide suitability. All prices/terms in the exercise must be labelled fictional.
Data-management purposeAn external provider may supply specialist operation, scale or continuity. The centre still needs accountable decisions, access rules, recovery, export and exit arrangements. Outsourcing does not automatically transfer every responsibility.
Software evaluationTest representative publishing/review/recovery tasks, usability, access needs, compatibility and support against the brief. Record observed results and unknowns; a feature list alone does not establish that the workflow works.

Fictional SLA calculation inputs and results

Check your reasoning

The support response target is 30 minutes. Is restoration within 30 minutes guaranteed?

Compare your explanation

No. Read each definition, covered hours and remedy; response and restoration can be different commitments.

Apply independently

Work through these steps for your own version of the community-centre brief. Retain your own output and actual observations, separately from the supplied model.

  1. Draft a fictional service summary with service type, availability window, counted/excluded downtime, response/restoration distinction, owner and escalation.
  2. Recalculate the worked example, then change the denominator to a stated business-hours window and explain why results cannot be compared without matching scope.
  3. Compare local/global outsourcing with in-house production against the same brief and explicit assumptions, including a data-management and exit requirement.
  4. Evaluate available software using the same representative tasks. Retain actual observations and explain an unresolved gap before making a qualified recommendation.

Use this within the existing chapter task, not an extra assessed project or additional teaching week. Record expected and actual results separately.

Evidence to retain

  • Availability arithmetic with units, window and exclusions.
  • Same-scope options comparison and data responsibility/exit record.
  • Actual evaluation observations separated from fictional SLA terms.

Actual learner performance, native application output and independent teacher review remain unverified. Supplied model results must not be copied into an observed-result log.
