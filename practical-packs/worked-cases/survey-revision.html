Turn evaluation evidence into a revision

Entirely fictional teaching dataset. No participants were surveyed. IDs, ratings, times, client feedback and retest observations are invented.

1. Define the check

The fictional brief requires a volunteer to identify the venue for the Repair session within 45 seconds, without a hint, using their assigned input method. Twelve invented volunteers attempt the before and after versions in the same order. The timer is capped at 60 seconds for an incomplete attempt. Ease is rated 1 (very difficult) to 5 (very easy); it is not a correctness score.

2. Work the calculation

Before: 8/12 complete (66.7%); after: 12/12 (100%). The increase is 33.3 percentage points, with four additional completions. Mean ease moves from 30/12 = 2.5 to 48/12 = 4.0. Include denominators and scale labels. Report the change as a description of these supplied records, not a proven effect in the population.

Median times across all attempts, including timeouts, are 30.5 and 21.5 seconds. If analysing only successful attempts, state that different denominator and recompute rather than silently dropping failures.

3. Connect evidence to a design decision

Two supplied notes show difficulty locating the venue. The fictional client requires the venue beside each session time. Move the venue from an ambiguously labelled More disclosure to a visible, labelled field; use the same information order across cards. The revised prototype implements that change. This decision uses task evidence and a client requirement, rather than treating the average rating as a design instruction.

4. Retest and qualify

The after rows meet this task criterion within the model. They do not prove the design is accessible to everyone. Participants repeat the same task, so familiarity may contribute; the group is small, and the records are fictional. Run your own approved trial, keep observations separate from the supplied model, and test another session and keyboard route after revising.

There are six keyboard records per version: 4/6 complete before and 6/6 after. These model results support a targeted follow-up; they do not replace keyboard, semantics, zoom or screen-reader checks.
