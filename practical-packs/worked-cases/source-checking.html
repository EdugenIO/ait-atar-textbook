Check a claim against its evidence

The centre, documents, dates and claims in this case are fictional.

Unit 4 managing data · Download the five-source register.

Question and evidence

You must publish the venue and start time. S1 is an older approved schedule naming Hall 1. S2 is the current approved revision naming Hall 2 at 10 am. S3 is a newer volunteer repost retaining Hall 1. S4 is a supplier’s unsupported productivity claim. S5 has the highest revision number but is an unapproved draft proposing Hall 3.

Worked decision

Publish Hall 2, 10 am, citing S2 and recording the approval status and check date. Currency includes whether a document has been superseded, not just its timestamp. Trace S3 back to its source: it repeats stale information. Keep S5 out of approved public content until the coordinator confirms it. Neither a professional layout nor HTTPS verifies the venue.

For S4, record who benefits from the claim, missing comparison conditions and absent measurement method. Ask for workload, device versions, participant selection and raw results. The source is verifiable as a supplier statement; that does not make the productivity claim accurate.

Guided practice

Which is more suitable evidence for the venue: the latest repost or the current approved schedule? Explain using authorship, original evidence and status, not a general rule that official sources are always right.

Compare reasoning

S2 is directly responsible for this case’s venue decision and is approved. S3 is later but derivative and inconsistent. If two approved sources conflict, pause and resolve the discrepancy rather than selecting by appearance.

Independent transfer

The coordinator approves S5. Update the publication decision and source log, list affected outputs and specify how you will check that the stale venue has been removed. Keep the previous decision as a versioned record. Then assess one real teacher-approved claim using two primary sources; record access/publication dates and what remains unverified.
