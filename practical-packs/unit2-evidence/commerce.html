Evaluate online retail and communication dependence

Unit 2 · fictional bicycle-shop client brief. Read the chapter explanation first. These are worked decisions, not observed learner results.

Worked decisions

FocusDecision and explanation
Improved communicationOnline product/service details can reach customers without a shop visit; accuracy, accessible content and response ownership still matter.
24/7 communicationA request can arrive outside business hours. Publish the actual response window instead of implying staff are always available.
Retail opportunitiesOnline booking/retail can extend reach but creates payment, fulfilment, support and stock-information dependencies. A broader market is not guaranteed profit.
Dependence on electronic/visual communicationAn image-only hours notice can exclude a reader; an outage can stop online booking. Provide equivalent text and an approved offline/contact fallback, then reconcile records when service returns.

Apply independently

Recommend a small online communication/retail improvement for the shop. Explain a benefit, operating cost or dependency, actual staff response expectations and an accessible fallback. Write an outage plan and a check preventing duplicate bookings across channels.

Use this within the existing chapter task, not an extra assessed project or additional teaching week. Record expected and actual results separately.

Evidence to retain

  • Separate service availability from staff response time.
  • Support a productivity claim with workload evidence rather than assuming online is always faster.
  • Evaluate the user outcome and operational burden together.

Actual learner performance, native application output and independent teacher review remain unverified. Supplied model results must not be copied into an observed-result log.
