Worked decisions

Fictional repair café brief: residents must find what to bring, the date, venue and next action. The decisions below model reasoning; they are not observed learner results.

FocusDecision and explanation
BalanceA large illustration on the left is counterbalanced by the dark information panel on the right; equal column widths are unnecessary.
Emphasis: contrast and proportionIncrease the date relative to supporting text and give it a stronger light–dark difference; explain both size relationship and contrast.
DominanceThe event heading has the greatest prominence across the composition; the date is emphasised but secondary.
Unity: proximity and repetitionKeep date and venue close, and repeat the same label treatment; grouping and repeated treatment provide different links.
PatternRepeat a small geometric motif at regular intervals in the border; keep it distinct from meaningful content groups.
MovementHeading position, divider direction and grouped details guide the viewing sequence toward the action; no animation is needed.

Apply independently

First identify and explain these principles in the supplied original poster. Then make two layouts using identical content. Change visual weight and dominance, and annotate contrast, proportion, proximity, repetition, pattern and viewing sequence. Ask a partner to locate the venue without pointing; revise one issue.

Use this as part of the existing chapter task, not an additional assessed project or extra lesson. Predict outcomes, create/inspect the required output and record the actual result.

Evidence to retain

  • Connect each principle to a visible element and a client need.
  • Do not equate balance with symmetry or movement with animation.
  • Keep intended effects separate from the partner observation.
