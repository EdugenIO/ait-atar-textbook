# AIT textbook content and accuracy audit

17 October 2026 · Publication baseline e39ed46 (edition 0.18); audit corrections prepared as edition 0.19.

## Outcome

All 24 reading chapters now have authored drafts, covering destinations in all 20 syllabus content areas. The reviewed material contains substantial explanations and optional practice, but this is **not a certification that every syllabus requirement has sufficient teaching depth**. Practical instruction is the main remaining weakness. No learning outcomes or accessibility conformance were independently verified.

The accompanying [checkpoint inventory](2026-10-17-requirements.csv) has 185 paraphrased knowledge/skill checkpoints. These are local review groupings, not official SCSA codes or a completion percentage. Related syllabus sub-items are sometimes grouped. Chapter links are area-level evidence locations, not a claim that every linked paragraph fully teaches every checkpoint. Each row retains its limitation and next action.

## Scope and method

Reviewed the authored lesson sections, worked examples, activities, answer text, teaching diagrams and illustration placements against the content on printed pages 7–13 of Year 11 (2026) and 7–12 of Year 12 (2027). Reviewed the 20-area register, glossary generation, prerequisites, chapter/session links, local tutorial and workbook exports. Rechecked arithmetic and selected legal/security claims with primary guidance. Ran the existing integrity, learning and print-click checks. Browser checks cover all chapter routes, selected interactions, image loading and narrow-layout overflow. External references were spot-checked, not exhaustively link-checked; real classroom trials, all browser/assistive-technology combinations and production network/service configurations are outside this audit.

Sources: [SCSA Year 11, teaching from 2026](https://senior-secondary.scsa.wa.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/1232466/Applied-Information-Technology-ATAR-Year-11-Syllabus-for-teaching-from-2026.PDF) and [SCSA Year 12, teaching from 2027](https://senior-secondary.scsa.wa.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/1273182/Applied-Information-Technology-ATAR-Year-12-Syllabus-for-teaching-from-2027.PDF). Official wording sometimes groups concepts differently: the textbook correctly distinguishes physical topology from service organisation, modern TLS from legacy SSL terminology, and the 802.11 family from 802.1X. The syllabus reference and the modern clarification should both remain visible.

## Prioritised remaining work

1. **Practical starter packs:** supply a short original audio clip, image masters, a timeline starter, template files and expected exports for Units 1–3. Add reproducible tool-neutral steps with a supported classroom-tool example. Distinguish doing an operation from analysing a description of it.
2. **Approved maintenance lab:** provide a disposable/simulated utility report plus a supervised lab that demonstrates the required tool operation. Keep real system deletion and protection changes out of student instructions.
3. **Online tutorial/form workflow:** retain the no-storage public demo, but add an approved classroom-only exercise showing sharing, a fictional response, receiving-side validation, response access, export and deletion. The public textbook itself does not need accounts or response collection.
4. **Worked emerging-device and global cases:** add dated primary-source device comparisons and a clearly bounded content-restriction case. The existing research prompts alone do not teach a complete worked comparison.
5. **Web application depth:** add an internationalisation example (language, dates and layout), a concretely defined Web 3.0 example, and a second-browser/assistive-technology test record. Do not infer conformance from absence of overflow or a single user trial.
6. **Independent review:** a subject teacher should check each inventory grouping, actual practical exemplars and pacing against the current syllabus. Review legal/device claims on a recorded schedule. No completion badge or coverage percentage should be added before that work.

## Accuracy checks and sources

- VLOOKUP/HLOOKUP examples: exact-match keys and row/column offsets checked against supplied tables; missing X99 remains an error, not an invented zero.
- Transfer arithmetic: 40 MB at 8 Mb/s = 40 s, 80 MB at 20 Mb/s = 32 s; both are ideal decimal estimates with overhead limitations.
- Animation: 24 frames at 12 fps = 2 s.
- Backup example: simple incremental recovery requires full + each necessary increment; differential requires full + selected differential. Sync and archive remain separate concepts.
- Availability arithmetic: 30 days = 43,200 min; 99.9% gives 43.2 min and 99.95% gives 21.6 min in the stated simplified model. Response time is not restoration time.
- [IP Australia: design-right process](https://www.ipaustralia.gov.au/design-rights/how-to-apply-for-a-design-right) supports the certification correction.
- [OAIC: small business](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/small-business), [workplace monitoring](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/surveillance-and-monitoring/workplace-monitoring-and-surveillance), [employee records](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/organisations/employee-records-exemption) and [cross-border handling](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-8-app-8-cross-border-disclosure-of-personal-information) support the qualified treatment; no blanket coverage or legality claim is made.
- [IETF TCP specification](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9293.html), [IEEE 802 groups](https://ieee802.org/) and [MDN TLS](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Defenses/Transport_Layer_Security) support the protocol/standard distinctions. HTTP/3 is explicitly distinguished from the assumed TCP-based example.
- [ASD cloud resources](https://www.cyber.gov.au/cloud-computing), [W3C forms guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/), [media guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/media/av/) and [user evaluation guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/) support the separated responsibilities and checks.

## Learning and product consistency

Each of the 24 drafts has goals, recall prompts, vocabulary links, a worked model, annotations, two section diagrams, a main visual walkthrough, a contextual illustration, guided/independent practice, a mistake correction, a partially completed example, a fresh context, an explained quick check and recap. These are authored learning supports, not evidence of learning effectiveness. Several prompts repeat by design; classroom review should trim repetition where it crowds out practical work. Text readability is an editorial judgement, not a measured Year 11/12 reading-level certification.

Student downloads remove answer panels rather than merely hiding them, while worked models remain. Illustration data is embedded for offline use. There are no site accounts, marks, submission storage or progress tracking. The local demo checks fictional values without a receiving service. Some software-use skills remain classroom activities requiring an approved available tool; the public no-account design does not itself demonstrate those skills.

## Review conclusion

The textbook is ready for continued draft use and targeted practical development. The next substantive task is the practical starter pack, not another layer of generic diagrams. Keep the draft/review labels until the remaining practical evidence and independent review are complete.
