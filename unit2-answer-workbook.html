1. Files, compression and version control

Recall

  • Why keep an editable master as well as a delivery file?
  • What happens when you filter records rather than delete them?

Visual walkthrough

Keep the master; compare the copies

Pause and predict: Which copy is supported by the stated evidence?

Show the explanation

Copy A. B is smaller but its label cannot be read. Keep the master too.

Try a small change: A printed poster now needs a much larger output. Where should you start?

Compare your reasoning

Return to the master and the print requirements. Enlarging the reduced web copy cannot restore missing source detail.

Try it with support

A student reduces both image dimensions by half and predicts that the JPEG file will be exactly one quarter of its previous size. What is sound, and what needs checking?

Example reasoning: The pixel count becomes one quarter: half the width multiplied by half the height. The encoded JPEG size must be measured because compression and image content also affect it.

Practise and explain

Choose: A spreadsheet must be restored exactly after transfer. Which compression approach fits?

Use a lossless method and check that the restored file opens correctly. Lossy media compression is unsuitable for preserving arbitrary spreadsheet data.

Distinguish: A crop removes the shop’s opening hours. Is a smaller file enough to justify it?

No. The output has lost required content. Change the crop or another setting while preserving the communication goal.

Plan: Two staff members edit separate copies called final.docx. What would improve control?

Agree a shared editing location, responsibilities and version identifiers. Record approval and recovery options rather than relying on the word final.

Explain: Why should a maintenance plan identify whether a drive is an HDD or SSD?

The storage technologies behave differently. Choose supported, drive-aware maintenance rather than applying traditional hard-disk defragmentation indiscriminately.

Plan a safe file-delivery workflow

  1. Use an original or supplied fictional image. Keep the source unchanged.
  2. Create or analyse two delivery copies and record settings, size and whether important detail survives.
  3. Explain whether cropping, resampling or compression caused each change.
  4. Write a naming, editing and approval procedure for two staff members.
  5. Describe a restore test using a disposable sample, and distinguish sync from backup.
  6. Select maintenance actions for a supplied report and explain what should be checked before applying them.

Evidence to keep: An export comparison, approved-copy decision, version procedure and maintenance plan.

Example response: A defensible workflow preserves the master, records a controlled comparison and chooses an output that retains required detail. It gives staff clear editing and approval roles, tests recovery on a sample and checks maintenance categories before any action.

A fresh context: a restaurant menu

A small menu image loads quickly, but customers cannot read the prices. The editable master is still available.

Recommend an output change and a version/recovery check.

Compare with an example response

Export a clearer copy from the master and compare it at the intended screen size. Record its settings and approval. Confirm that a prior approved version can be restored using a disposable test.

Exit question

Explain why a smaller file and a synced folder do not, by themselves, prove a good file-management workflow.

2. Networks for a small business

Recall

  • What does a peripheral add to a computer system?
  • Which requirements would you ask a client before recommending equipment?

Visual walkthrough

One LAN: star links and shared services

Pause and predict: Does adding a file server stop this being a physical star?

Show the explanation

No. The server can connect to the switch like another endpoint. Client-server describes the service arrangement.

Try a small change: The central switch becomes unavailable. Which assumption must you revisit?

Compare your reasoning

Connectivity through that switch is lost for dependent devices. Review the failure dependency and the business’s continuity needs.

Try it with support

An 80 MB file crosses a link at an ideal constant 20 Mb/s. Calculate the ideal time and name one reason the real transfer could take longer.

Example reasoning: 80 × 8 = 640 megabits; 640 ÷ 20 = 32 seconds. Overhead, congestion or a slower part of the route could increase the time. This is an ideal estimate, not a measured result.

Practise and explain

Distinguish: What is the difference between a switch and a router?

A switch commonly connects local devices and forwards local traffic. A router forwards between networks. One appliance may combine roles.

Apply: Why might fixed desks use cable while a roaming laptop uses wireless?

The desks do not need mobility and can have dedicated physical links. Wireless supports movement, but coverage and shared capacity need checking.

Trace: What ideal time does a 10 MB file need at 8 Mb/s?

10 × 8 = 80 megabits. 80 ÷ 8 = 10 seconds before real-world overhead or other limits.

Evaluate: If the central switch fails, can the diagram guarantee normal local sharing?

No. Devices depending on that switch can lose connectivity. Identify the dependency and plan an appropriate response rather than promising uninterrupted service.

Propose a LAN for a small workplace

  1. List the print shop’s users, devices and services. Mark assumptions needing confirmation.
  2. Draw the wired star and label the switch, endpoints and link media.
  3. Add the laptop, access point, router and service boundary; explain combined device roles where relevant.
  4. Compare client-server and peer-to-peer sharing against the client’s needs.
  5. Calculate one ideal transfer time using the supplied file size and rate.
  6. Explain access controls, one failure dependency and the site/performance checks needed.

Evidence to keep: An annotated LAN proposal, comparison and rate calculation with limitations.

Example response: A defensible proposal uses wired links for fixed devices, a tested wireless option for mobility and a correctly labelled boundary. It justifies any server, states access and backup arrangements and acknowledges that capacity and coverage require checking.

A fresh context: a pop-up stall

Two staff use mobile tablets, and a fixed back-office computer manages records. The venue’s wireless conditions are unknown.

Recommend checks before promising reliable networking.

Compare with an example response

Check service needs, wireless coverage, interference, connection capacity and device support. Explain what can continue during an outage. Test representative work rather than assuming the advertised link rate is achieved.

Exit question

Explain how one LAN could use a star layout, wireless access and a file server at the same time.

3. Privacy and access to technology

Recall

  • Why are permission and attribution different checks?
  • Why might a private online group still need careful sharing decisions?

Visual walkthrough

Follow personal information through the service

Pause and predict: Does the booking purpose automatically authorise an unrelated marketing use?

Show the explanation

No. The new use needs its own check against the relevant purpose, notices, permissions and rules.

Try a small change: The online form stops working for a day. What must the alternative process preserve?

Compare your reasoning

A workable booking channel, appropriate handling of customer details and a way to reconcile temporary records without double bookings.

Try it with support

A customer used an email address for a booking reminder. A staff member wants to add it to a marketing list. What must be checked?

Example reasoning: The new use needs checking against the original purpose, notices, permissions and applicable rules. Do not assume a reminder contact authorises marketing. Refer a real decision to the business’s approved privacy process.

Practise and explain

Identify: Which mock booking fields would you question when there is no delivery service?

Ask why a home address or date of birth is needed. Retain only fields justified by the service and applicable requirements.

Explain: Why does removing names not always make a dataset anonymous?

Other fields or linked information may still identify someone. De-identification must consider the actual context and re-identification risk.

Apply: A customer cannot use the web form. Suggest an alternative and one coordination check.

Offer a phone or counter booking. Ensure staff update the same authorised booking records so channels do not create conflicting appointments.

Evaluate: Does an online store being open 24 hours guarantee instant staff support?

No. The business should distinguish service availability, automated messages and realistic staffed response times.

Review a fictional booking service

  1. Use invented records only. List the booking purpose and justify each requested field.
  2. Describe who needs access and how a request for a record would be checked.
  3. Identify one identity-theft risk and a matching protection.
  4. Write retention questions and a disposal plan for review, including possible copies.
  5. Identify two digital-access barriers and provide a practical alternative channel.
  6. Explain one benefit of online orders, one limitation and an outage communication plan.

Evidence to keep: A revised mock form, data-handling notes and an inclusive service plan.

Example response: A strong response removes unjustified fields, limits access and flags legal or retention questions for checking. It addresses recoverable copies, offers a coordinated alternative channel and states realistic communication expectations.

A fresh context: a community workshop

An online-only registration asks for identity documents without a stated reason. Some residents cannot use the form.

Identify one collection question and one access improvement.

Compare with an example response

Ask why the documents are needed and whether a less intrusive process can meet the purpose, subject to relevant obligations. Offer a coordinated phone or supported registration route without exposing identity records.

Exit question

Explain how a more convenient online service could still exclude customers or create a privacy risk.

4. Business documents and communication

Recall

  • Why keep an editable source as well as a delivery copy?
  • What makes an image suitable for the audience’s viewing size?

Visual walkthrough

From frames to a working customer journey

Pause and predict: What happens to the duration if the same 24 frames are played at 24 fps?

Show the explanation

The duration becomes 1 second. Doubling the playback frame rate halves the duration when the frame count stays fixed.

Try a small change: The screen becomes narrower. Which can change while the required information stays?

Compare your reasoning

The arrangement can change from side-by-side to stacked blocks. Keep the services, hours and useful navigation readable and operable.

Try it with support

A 24-frame sequence runs at 12 frames per second. A student changes it to 24 frames per second without adding frames. How does the duration change?

Example reasoning: It changes from 2 seconds to 1 second: frame count divided by frames per second. Check whether the faster movement still gives viewers enough time to understand the message.

Practise and explain

Compare: When could a tween be useful instead of drawing every intermediate position?

A predictable change such as moving an object from one position to another can use calculated intermediate states. Frame-by-frame work can give more direct control over individually drawn changes.

Distinguish: How do HTML and CSS contribute different things to the same page?

HTML describes the content and structure. CSS controls presentation. A useful site needs appropriate content and an arrangement that supports its use.

Debug: A Contact link points to contact.html, but the delivered file is called contacts.html. What should you check?

Make the intended filename and reference agree, including case where relevant, then follow the link in the delivered folder or hosted copy. Check the return route too.

Evaluate: An exported audio file plays, but the opening hours are hard to hear. Is playback alone a sufficient check?

No. The information must be intelligible. Review the recording, levels and export, listen again and provide the text equivalent.

Apply: Why might a narrow layout stack blocks rather than make all the text smaller?

Stacking preserves readable content while adapting the arrangement to the space. Confirm that the resulting order still supports the user’s task.

Build a small business communication prototype

  1. Use the fictional bicycle-shop brief. Write the message, required facts and two user tasks.
  2. Create a three-destination navigation map with return routes.
  3. Storyboard a 24-frame, 12-fps movement. Compare a frame-by-frame plan with a simple tween plan.
  4. Record or import a short fictional announcement, make an edit and preserve the project and original. Export and check a delivery copy with a transcript.
  5. Author a small linked prototype using approved tools. Label links, choose appropriate graphics and identify the HTML and CSS roles.
  6. Test narrow and wide layouts, every route and any control using keyboard and pointer. Record one change and its retest.

Evidence to keep: Navigation map, animation comparison, audio/project export notes, transcript, linked prototype and test record.

Example response: A defensible prototype gives clear routes between Home, Services and Contact, keeps required hours readable and provides a text equivalent for audio. The frame-count example lasts two seconds at 12 fps. Its test record separates successful playback from understandable content and confirms the actual delivery files.

A fresh context: a community theatre

A theatre prototype has a seat-map animation, a spoken announcement and a booking-information page. On a phone, the hours become tiny.

Suggest a layout revision and two separate delivery checks.

Compare with an example response

Stack content or simplify its arrangement while keeping readable text. Check the exported audio for understandable speech and a text equivalent; follow the booking-information route in the delivered prototype. Also test relevant controls rather than relying on their appearance.

Exit question

Describe one media or layout decision and the user task you would use to check it.

5. Developing a business solution

Recall

  • How is a testable criterion different from a vague preference?
  • Why should a team keep versions and approval decisions?

Visual walkthrough

Connect a requirement to proof

Pause and predict: If a task is finished but the user cannot find the hours, is the requirement passed?

Show the explanation

No. The task completion is not enough. Record the failed observation, revise the relevant part and retest.

Try a small change: The client changes the hours after approval. What else must be revisited?

Compare your reasoning

Update the approved content and any repeated versions, record the change and retest the affected output. Do not assume an earlier result covers changed content.

Try it with support

An optional animation is unfinished, and testing reveals that the Services link is broken. Which should be prioritised, and why?

Example reasoning: Repair and retest the required route first because it blocks a user task. Review the optional animation against the remaining time and agreed scope. Record the decision rather than silently changing the promise.

Practise and explain

Identify: Give one dependency in a small business prototype.

Final opening-hours content depends on receiving and confirming the hours. A layout draft can often proceed with labelled placeholders.

Improve: Rewrite “make a good website” as a checkable requirement.

For example: a visitor can navigate from Home to the repair-service details and back using the provided links. State the intended context and how you will observe it.

Plan: What should a team do if its chosen application cannot open a supplied asset?

Record the compatibility issue, check an approved conversion or supported alternative, and revise the task/resources plan. Retest the delivery output.

Evaluate: Why should “not tested” remain distinct from “passed”?

There is no supporting observation for an unperformed check. Treating it as passed creates false confidence and hides remaining work.

Reflect: A client likes the colours but trial users miss the opening hours. What evidence belongs in the evaluation?

Record both the positive preference feedback and the failed information-finding task. Revise the hours treatment and retest the relevant requirement.

Plan, build and review a small business solution

  1. Use the fictional bicycle-shop problem and define a manageable prototype scope.
  2. Write at least three requirements with a check for each; identify unresolved client questions.
  3. Create an action plan with task owners, resources, dependencies and review points.
  4. Build the prototype in small steps, using original or approved assets and fictional details.
  5. Test required routes, information-finding tasks and narrow/wide presentation. Record failures and untested areas honestly.
  6. Revise a meaningful issue, repeat its test and prepare a handover with evidence and limitations.

Evidence to keep: Requirements, action plan, prototype, test/revision record and a short evidence-based evaluation.

Example response: A strong response gives each requirement a practical check, prioritises working information routes and records dependencies such as content approval. Its evaluation explains a revision using observed difficulty and a retest. It includes source files, delivery files and known limitations without claiming an untested production system.

A fresh context: a mobile pet-grooming service

The client wants an information prototype by Friday. The supplied service area has not been confirmed, but a decorative video is ready.

Identify the dependency, a useful task that can proceed and the priority before handover.

Compare with an example response

Confirm the service area before approving its final content. Build and test navigation using labelled placeholders while waiting. Prioritise accurate service information and working routes over optional decoration, then record approval and retest the changed output.

Exit question

Choose one requirement and explain its dependent task, resource and test.
